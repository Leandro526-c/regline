Your lab measurements, from table to report.

Paste your measurements, fit a line or your own formula, propagate uncertainties and export graphs ready for your report. No fiddling with Excel, no programming.

The real app, recorded as is: a table copied from Excel becomes a graph with its fit, uncertainties and χ² in a few seconds.

1

Paste or type your data

From Excel, a CSV, TXT, .xlsx or .xls file, a LabVIEW file, or straight into the table. ΔX and ΔY columns are recognised automatically, and even a slightly messy file still imports.

2

Choose your model

Straight line, line through the origin, a model from the library or your own formula. The fit updates with every change.

3

Take everything to your report

Parameters rounded to their uncertainties, χ², graph as PNG, SVG or PDF, LaTeX table ready to compile.

The whole lab session in one window

Made for lab courses, from high school to university.

Physics, chemistry, biology or earth sciences: Regline does the repetitive calculations and leaves you free to understand what you measure.

A table like in Excel

Paste from Excel, type “=1/2” in a cell, sort and filter without deleting anything. One table can feed several graphs: fix a value once and it's fixed everywhere.

Fit any model

Straight lines, your own formula, a model library (physics, chemistry, biology), differential equations and global fits across several curves.

Uncertainties done right

ΔX and ΔY, propagation by partial derivatives, Monte Carlo check, uncertainty budget. Results rounded to significant figures.

Readable formulas

Type as in Excel or on your calculator. Regline shows the formula as real maths and flags mistakes as you type, even forgotten degrees.

Statistical tests, explained

χ², normalised deviation, Welch, ANOVA, Mann-Whitney, contingency χ². A glossary explains each test in plain words.

Every graph a lab needs

Residuals, ±1σ band, two Y axes, Bode plots, histograms, box plots, bars ± SEM, multiple panels and annotations.

Your report, faster

Notes exported to PDF or LaTeX, ready-to-compile LaTeX tables, pgfplots, PNG, SVG or PDF images, copy for Word and Excel, and the Python matplotlib code or a Jupyter notebook if you want it.

Light, offline, in 5 languages

Your data stays on your computer. In Français, English, Deutsch, Italiano and Español, on Windows, Mac and Linux.

Video analysis

Film a motion with your phone and send the video by QR code. Calibrate on a visible ruler, click the object or let Regline track it: the t, x, y table arrives with its uncertainties.

Read an image

Take the points from a photographed or scanned graph, even tilted or on a log scale, or measure a distance and an angle on a photo of your setup.

The report during the lab

Notes become a real document: headings, images, formulas, and tables and graphs that keep updating. Export to PDF, Markdown or LaTeX (a ready-to-compile .zip), with your course's template.

Live measurements

Read an Arduino over its USB cable, follow a file while it is being written or open LabVIEW files. New and still little tested on real hardware: lab instruments (VISA) and National Instruments boards. Your feedback is welcome!

More complete fits

Counts (Poisson), χ² profile, fixed or linked parameters, “stat ± syst” uncertainties, peak series and z = f(x, y) maps. Regline also flags a formula whose units do not add up.

A real spreadsheet

Duplicate a table to redo the same lab with other measurements, cut, find and replace, click a column to write it into a formula. Old .xls files and very large files open too.

Presentation mode

Press F5: full screen with only your graph and your table, to show your result on the projector.

Nicer everywhere

Consistent windows, “Undo” after every deletion, a history of steps, zoom with Ctrl + scroll wheel, a shortcuts sheet and a one-minute guided tour.

Formulas

  • Live maths preview: you see straight away whether the formula is the one you meant.
  • Mistakes caught as you type: a missing bracket, an unknown parameter, an angle in degrees instead of radians.

Installation

Installed in a minute.

Regline then updates itself, at most once a day (you can turn this off in Settings).

  1. Easiest: open Regline on the Microsoft Store and click Install. No warning, and updates happen automatically.
  2. If the Store is blocked (for example on a university PC), download the installer instead and open it. If Windows says “Windows protected your PC”, click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Regline then appears in the Start menu.

The installer warning appears because Regline is independent software, not signed by a publisher. No administrator rights needed.

  1. Download the version for your Mac. To find out which: Apple menu → About This Mac. “Chip Apple M…”: Apple Silicon. “Processor Intel”: Intel.
  2. Open the .dmg file and drag Regline into the Applications folder.
  3. On first launch macOS blocks the app: close the message, open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway at the bottom.

If macOS says the app is “damaged”, open Terminal and type: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Regline.app

  1. Download the Linux archive and extract it: tar -xzf Regline-*-Linux-x86_64.tar.gz
  2. Run Regline/Regline

On Ubuntu or Debian, if nothing opens: sudo apt install libxcb-cursor0

Questions

What people often ask me.

Is it really free?

Yes. No ads, no account, no paid version. If Regline helps you, you can support the project with a donation, but it is never required.

Does my data leave my computer?

No. Your measurements stay on your computer. Regline only connects to the internet to check for updates (at most once a day, can be turned off) and, if you analyse a video, to download the video module once.

Do I need to know how to code?

No, everything is done with mouse and keyboard. If you want to go further, Regline can generate the Python matplotlib code for your graph.

Does it replace Excel?

For lab work, yes: table, formulas in cells, sorting, filters, graphs and fits with uncertainties. You can always export your data to .xlsx.

Does it work for chemistry or biology?

Yes. The library includes Michaelis-Menten, Hill, Arrhenius, logistic growth, Beer-Lambert and more, and the statistical tests work in every science.

Why does Windows or macOS warn me?

Regline is developed by a student, without a paid publisher certificate. The Installation section explains how to open it anyway.

Do my old .regline files still open?

Yes. Files from previous versions open in 4.3 as they are.

What do I need for video analysis?

A video (MP4, MOV…), for example filmed with your phone, and an object of known length visible in the picture, such as a ruler, for calibration. To read the frames, Regline uses the open-source program FFmpeg: the version downloaded here offers to download it the first time you open a video, and the Microsoft Store version already includes it.

I found a bug or I have an idea.

Write to me at the address at the bottom of the page, ideally with a screenshot or your .regline file. Every message is read.
